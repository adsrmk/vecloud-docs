The **SSL certificates** card in **Overview** helps locate domains with possible HTTPS problems. Use this guide when the new card is available in your account.

The checker makes a public TLS connection to the primary domain of each included website. It checks the certificate chain, hostname and validity dates. It is not a complete scan of all aliases or subdomains.

## Run a check

1. Open **Overview** and find **SSL certificates**.
2. Wait for results, or use **Check SSL** to run a fresh check. Earlier results may be reused for a few minutes.
3. Open a category containing results to see its domains and available expiry dates.
4. Select a domain to open that website’s **SSL certificates** page.

## Understand the statuses

| Status | Meaning and next step |
| --- | --- |
| Valid / Active | The publicly presented certificate passes the check. |
| Expiring soon | Still valid, but expires within seven days. Check renewal. |
| Invalid or missing | A certificate problem was identified, such as trust, hostname or validity. Inspect that domain. |
| Not checked | No reliable outcome was available, for example due to DNS, connectivity or a timeout. Check reachability and try again later. |

Expiring certificates are also included in **Websites with valid SSL**. Do not add these categories as if they represented separate websites.

## Check the relevant certificate

With Cloudflare or another proxy, the checker may see that proxy’s certificate. A valid public certificate then does not prove that the connection between proxy and hosting is also configured correctly.

The check does not request or automatically renew certificates. For installation, renewal and **Enforce HTTPS**, follow [the SSL guide](./ssl.mdx). For **Not checked**, start with [active DNS](./index.mdx); repeatedly requesting a certificate does not resolve an unreachable domain.
