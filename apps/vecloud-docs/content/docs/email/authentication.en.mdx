title: Check email authentication
description: Align SPF, DKIM and DMARC with the services sending on behalf of your domain.

For dependable outgoing email, the recipient needs to check who is sending on behalf of your domain. Start by listing your mailbox provider, website forms and any other sending services.

## Identify the sending routes

For each service, record the sending domain and where its DNS records are managed. A mailbox with an external provider and a form on your hosting may use different sending routes.

Also review [mail routing and MX](../dns-ssl/mail-routing.mdx). Those control incoming delivery; the authentication checks below concern outgoing mail.

## Check SPF

Use one TXT record with `v=spf1` per name. Combine the values supplied by your sending services. Multiple SPF policies for the same name cause an error. See the [SPF specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208).

Use values from services you actually use. An example containing `mx` or an arbitrary `include` does not automatically cover website forms, newsletters and external mailboxes. Preserve the previous value before changing the policy.

## Enable DKIM for the appropriate sender

1. Open **Domains → your website → DNS settings** and find **Email authentication**.
2. Enable DKIM when this hosting sends the relevant mail.
3. Check validation. If unvalidated, use the copy actions for `hostingcp._domainkey` and the complete TXT value.
4. Publish that value at the active DNS provider and check again after DNS updates.

**Verified** confirms the displayed DNS check. If an external provider sends your messages, also configure its own DKIM; the switch does not configure that external service.

## Add an appropriate DMARC policy

DMARC requires aligned SPF or DKIM. Publish its TXT record at `_dmarc`; `p=none` requests monitoring. Discuss reporting and enforcement with your provider. See the [specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989.html).

## Test real messages

Send separately from your mailbox and a website form. At the recipient, inspect message headers including `Authentication-Results` and check SPF, DKIM and DMARC outcomes. A green DKIM status in the panel does not automatically test every sending route.

If messages are still missing, review [spam settings](./spam.mdx), delivery errors and the sending application’s configuration. Give support the time, sender, recipient and error code through an appropriate channel; do not share SMTP passwords or private keys.
