Resource charts show the capacity used by one website environment. Compare a peak with traffic, updates and scheduled jobs to put it in context.

## Choose a chart and period

Open **Domains → your website → Analytics** and find **Resource usage**. Select **CPU**, **Memory**, **Storage performance (I/O)** or **Processes**. The periods are **24h**, **1w** and **1 month**, with 24 hours as the default. This selection is separate from the traffic charts above.

**Last refreshed** shows when readings were retrieved.

## What does each tab measure?

| Measurement | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| CPU | Processor use; 100% represents one vCPU. |
| Memory | Website-environment memory, rather than only PHP memory_limit. |
| Storage performance (I/O) | Bytes read and written per second. |
| Processes | Observed number of processes. |

Longer histories are grouped into intervals: 30 minutes for 24h, 3 hours for 1w and 12 hours for a month. Lines show time-weighted averages; tooltips can also show peaks. A brief peak may therefore exceed the average line. Short histories retain their original readings.

## Limits and missing readings

Where available, the latest reading’s limit appears below the chart. **Unlimited** means no specific limit was recorded, not that the physical server has unlimited capacity.

Missing or unusable readings remain gaps instead of being filled with zeros. For an empty chart, check the website, period and message. A loading error does not automatically mean the website is offline.

## Turn a reading into action

Test one adjustment at a time, then compare the same period. Charts do not change limits. Discuss a required [hosting adjustment with support](../websites/advanced.mdx). With an assigned dedicated server, [Server metrics](./server-metrics.mdx) adds an overview of that whole server.
